Every food service establishment in New York City receives an unannounced, onsite inspection at least once a year to check if it is meeting Health Code food safety requirements.
Each violation is associated with a range of points depending on the type and extent of the violation, and the risk it poses to the public.
Lower inspection scores indicate better compliance with the Health Code.
Health Code violations are classified as “critical” or “general”.
Critical violations are given more points than general violations.
Pre-permit serious items” are critical violations in the design of an establishment, such as improper sewage disposal or lack of a hand-washing facility near the food preparation area.
The Health Department will not issue a permit if the establishment has one of these conditions.
The number of points an establishment receives for a critical or general violation depends on its condition level, meaning the extent and frequency of the violation.
Some cited violations may result in a Notice of Violation, fine and/or follow-up inspection, but are not counted toward the inspection score.
A restaurant may also be closed if it scores 28 or more points on three consecutive inspections.
If it is in sufficient compliance, it may remain open and will be inspected again in about three months.
As of July 2010, certain types of food service establishments, including restaurants, coffee shops, bars, nightclubs and most cafeterias and fixed-site food stands, must post letter grades that correspond to their sanitary inspection scores.
A score of 0-13 results in a grade of A; 14-27 points, a B; and 28 or more points, a C.
Restaurants with A grades are inspected less often than those with B or C grades.
Every food service establishment is scheduled for at least one inspection per year.
A restaurant that scores 0 to 13 violation points on its first inspection will receive an A-grade card that must be posted immediately.
If an A is issued on re-inspection, the A-grade card must be posted immediately.
