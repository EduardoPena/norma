\subsection{Constraint Mapping Performance}
\label{sec:constraintextraction}

Our second experiment evaluates how model and mapper choices affect mapping
quality. We evaluate mapping quality using \emph{constraint extraction} F$_1$ and \emph{aggregate
violation} F$_1$ as metrics. To isolate mapper quality from
classification errors, both metrics are computed only on the ground-truth
constraint sentences. We \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{focus first on the sentence-level setting and }\DIFdelend compare the \emph{Single-Prompt} and \emph{Multi-Step}
mappers across six LLMs on all \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{four }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{six }\DIFaddend datasets.

Figure~\ref{fig:mapper-results} summarizes the results. \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{On the four original
datasets, }\DIFaddend \emph{Multi-Step} \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{again }\DIFaddend improves both constraint extraction and
aggregate violation recovery over \emph{Single-Prompt} \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, with the largest gains }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{(mean over models:
0.80 vs.\ 0.75 and 0.89 vs.\ 0.55). The largest gains are }\DIFaddend on Restaurant and
SISU\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. These datasets contain more rules that require
combining several conditions into a single DC, making the decomposition steps of }\emph{\DIFdel{Multi-Step}} %DIFAUXCMD
\DIFdel{especially useful}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, whose rules combine several conditions or relate two tuples}\DIFaddend . Hospital and
Tax are \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{substantially easier }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{easy }\DIFaddend for most configurations\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. With the current backbones, the best
Multi-Step configurations exceed the best configuration of our previous
evaluation (GPT-5}\DIFaddend , \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{leaving less room for
improvement. We also observe that task decomposition improves robustness for
models that are less stable }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{0.78 extraction and 0.95 violation): Claude Sonnet 5.5 reaches
0.84 and 0.97, and GPT-6 Sol and Mistral Large 4 0.81 and 0.99. Decomposition also keeps the mapper
usable when a backbone is unstable }\DIFaddend under the single-prompt formulation\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. This effect is
particularly evident  for Qwen3-32B across datasets and for both Mistral
variants
on SISU.

\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Even the best configuration remains far from perfect on constraint extraction,
especially on SISU and Restaurant . Part of this gap is
due to the strictness of the metric. As in exact-match evaluation for Text-to-SQL~}%DIFDELCMD < \cite{yu2018spider}%%%
\DIFdel{, predictions can be penalized despite being close in meaning to the reference. Many extracted DCs are structurally close }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \smallskip\noindent\textbf{\DIFadd{Where Single-Prompt loses violations.}}
\DIFadd{The aggregate gap on Restaurant and SISU has two distinct causes. On Restaurant,
one rule (at least one inspection per year) flags 99\% of the gold-violating
tuples. Every Single-Prompt configuration except Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B returns
}\texttt{\DIFadd{null}} \DIFadd{for it, so aggregate recall falls to 0.01. Once this rule is
excluded, all of them reach 1.00. On SISU, the Single-Prompt mapper identifies
the student by the exam registration number instead of the national ID in the
two-tuple rules. The resulting DCs flag about 51k tuples instead of 533
(precision below 0.01), although most single-tuple rules are correct. Multi-Step avoids both errors because it links columns
and selects predicates before writing the DC.
}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{\DIFadd{HMDA datasets.}}
\DIFadd{The two HMDA datasets remain the hardest. Exact extraction stays low (at most
0.24 on HMDAZ and 0.68 on HMDA Checks), mainly for two reasons. Many predicted
DCs are logically equivalent }\DIFaddend to the gold \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{constraint but still
counted as errors because they differ slightly in a literal, an operator}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{ones but written differently, e.g.,
}\texttt{\DIFadd{IN}} \DIFadd{instead of a disjunction}\DIFaddend , or a \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{boundary condition. This also makes aggregate violation a useful complementary metric: some predictions that miss exact structural credit still recover the intended violations reasonably well.
}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{label column instead of its code
column. Others omit the coverage conditions of a trigger. Equivalence-aware
extraction (Table~\ref{tab:mapping-metrics}) recovers a large part of the first
group: on the HMDA datasets it raises the mean extraction F$_1$ from 0.36 to
0.49 for Multi-Step and from 0.24 to 0.51 for Single-Prompt. On the four original
datasets it is almost identical to exact extraction. The aggregate violation
ranking is also reversed on HMDA Checks: Single-Prompt reaches 0.99, while
Multi-Step stays at 0.10--0.16. This reversal says little about mapping quality.
Two date-range edits (S270 and Q022) flag 99\% of the gold-violating tuples, which
cover 98.6\% of the rows, so a DC that flags }\emph{\DIFadd{every}} \DIFadd{tuple already scores 0.99.
These are the only gold DCs that need arithmetic over dates encoded as integers.
Multi-Step returns }\texttt{\DIFadd{null}} \DIFadd{for both with every backbone, consistent with its
precision bias. Single-Prompt writes DCs for them, but most of these DCs flag all
rows (e.g., }\texttt{\DIFadd{t1.action\_taken\_date / 10000 != t1.activity\_year}}\DIFadd{, which
never holds as an equality under real division); only GPT-6 Luna, GPT-6 Sol, and
Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B produce an execution-equivalent DC for one of the two. Without these
two edits, the ranking flips back: Multi-Step reaches 1.00 with Claude Sonnet 5.5,
GPT-6 Luna, and GPT-6 Sol and 0.77 with the other three backbones (mean 0.89),
whereas Single-Prompt ranges from 0.18 to 1.00 (mean 0.65). Per-rule metrics,
which weigh every rule equally, are far less sensitive to these two edits: on
HMDA Checks, the mean per-rule violation F$_1$ is 0.80 for Multi-Step and 0.86 for
Single-Prompt with all edits, and 0.87 and 0.85 without the date edits. On the
remaining rules, the two mappers are therefore close, and the aggregate
reversal does not reflect the overall quality of the rules.
}\DIFaddend
